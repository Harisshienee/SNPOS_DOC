# SNPOS — Speaker Notes
### Conference Presentation | 18 Slides | April 2026

---

## Slide 1 — SNPOS: Bridging Nutrition Science and Academic Excellence

Before showing this slide, open with a direct question to the audience: *"When was the last time a teacher knew what a student ate before their exam?"* Let that sit for a moment. This reframes the audience's mental model before the first word of the presentation. SNPOS is not an upgrade to an existing tool — it is an entirely new category of school system. The tagline "making the invisible visible" should be read aloud slowly. It is the thesis of everything that follows.

**Transition:** "Before we show you what SNPOS does, let us show you the world it was built to fix."

---

## Slide 2 — One Invisible Variable Is Limiting Every Sri Lankan Student *(Executive Summary)*

Use this slide as the anchor and map. Every subsequent slide expands one of these four cells in detail. If you have only five minutes with a decision-maker, this is the only slide you need. Point to each quadrant as you speak — Problem, Solution, Evidence, Action — and make eye contact. The 12% figure in the Evidence cell is your hook; do not explain it yet, simply let it register as a number worth returning to.

**Transition:** "To understand why this system had to be built, we need to understand the environment it serves."

---

## Slide 3 — Sri Lankan Students Face Asia's Highest-Stakes Exam Pressure *(Background)*

Paint the daily reality of a Sri Lankan student in vivid, concrete terms. Not abstract statistics — the lived experience of millions of children: arriving at school before 7:30 AM, attending tuition classes until 6 PM or later, going eight to ten hours without a balanced meal. The O-Level and A-Level examinations are not simply tests — they are the sole determinants of university access and social mobility in Sri Lanka. The iron deficiency statistic is particularly important for audiences unfamiliar with South Asian adolescent health: it is not a risk factor, it is a prevalent condition with a documented cognitive impact.

**Transition:** "In this environment, what happens when nutrition falters — and nobody notices?"

---

## Slide 4 — No School in Sri Lanka Can See Nutrition's Impact on Grades *(Problem)*

This is the most emotionally important slide in the presentation. Read the three scenarios — the teacher, the parent, the administrator — slowly, with deliberate pauses between each. Do not rush to the neuroscience evidence. Let the audience sit with the recognition that these scenarios describe schools they know, teachers they know, students they know. The power of this slide is in the recognition it produces, not the data it presents. The data on the right-hand panel validates the scenarios; do not lead with it.

**Transition:** "The tragedy is not that this data doesn't exist — it's that no tool has ever brought the two data streams together."

---

## Slide 5 — Existing Tools Are Siloed and Blind to Nutrition-Performance Links *(Research Gap)*

Direct the audience's attention to the Venn diagram. Point to the empty intersection and hold there: "SNPOS lives here. Nothing else does." The three surrounding circles represent real, established product categories — apps your audience has heard of, systems their schools may use. The point is not that those tools are bad; it is that none of them were designed to occupy this intersection. SNPOS does not compete with MyFitnessPal or a school management system. It fills a structurally empty space.

**Transition:** "The gap is precisely identified. The question is: what does it take to fill it?"

---

## Slide 6 — Students Fail Exams Partly Because Nobody Monitors Their Nutrition *(Problem Statement)*

Let the pull-quote fill the screen for five full seconds before speaking. Allow the audience to read it themselves. This is the thesis statement of the entire presentation — a single sentence that contains the problem, the population, and the stakes. After the pause, reinforce it with the three statistic callouts at the bottom: zero schools currently tracking this, O-Level as an irreversible gateway, and 2026 as the target year. The quote should be read aloud exactly as written — do not paraphrase it.

**Transition:** "To solve a precise problem, SNPOS was built around four precise research questions."

---

## Slide 7 — Four Questions Define What SNPOS Was Built to Answer *(Research Questions)*

Read each question and immediately explain which system feature it generated. Question 1 produced the Sri Lanka Food Database. Question 2 produced the Pearson correlation engine. Question 3 produced the Early Warning System. Question 4 produced the PWA architecture, PDPA compliance, and 3G optimisation. This mapping demonstrates that every design decision in SNPOS is evidence-grounded, not intuitive — which is a critical argument for an academic or innovation award audience.

**Transition:** "Those four questions demanded a conceptual framework grounded in established nutritional neuroscience."

---

## Slide 8 — Nutritional Neuroscience Drives SNPOS's Analytical Design *(Conceptual Framework)*

Walk through the flow diagram left to right without rushing. The neuroscience base is not decoration — it is the justification for everything the analytics engine does. SNPOS is not a technology project that happens to involve food. It is a nutritional neuroscience intervention delivered through technology. Emphasise the privacy tier panel on the right: the distinction between what students see, what teachers see, and what administrators see is not a technical detail — it is the ethical architecture that makes the system trustworthy enough for adolescents to use.

**Transition:** "Here is how that framework was translated into a functioning system."

---

## Slide 9 — SNPOS Is Built for 3G, Local Food, and Sri Lanka's PDPA *(Methodology)*

Tailor this slide to your audience. For a technical panel: dwell on the security stack — TLS 1.3, AES-256, JWT RS256, rate limiting, parameterised queries. For a school administrator or policy audience: focus on "works on any phone, no download needed, runs on 3G." The PDPA compliance callout should prompt a nod in any Sri Lankan audience — data privacy for student health records is non-negotiable, and the fact that SNPOS was designed from the ground up to comply with No. 9 of 2022 is a significant differentiator from any imported tool.

**Transition:** "The architecture is only as valuable as the data flowing through it — which brings us to how SNPOS captures that data."

---

## Slide 10 — Two Portals Feed One Engine No School System Has Had Before *(Data Collection)*

Emphasise the privacy boundary emphatically: individual meal logs are visible to the student only. Teachers see aggregated nutritional indicators — never the specific meal a student ate at 7 AM. This matters because the system depends on student trust to function. If students believe their meal data can be inspected by teachers, they will not log consistently, the data will be poor, and the correlations will be meaningless. Privacy-by-design is not a compliance checkbox — it is the foundational condition for the entire system's value.

**Transition:** "Let us now look at what that engine produces."

---

## Slide 11 — SNPOS Generates Insight at Individual, Class, School, and Policy Level *(Results Overview)*

Start at the base of the pyramid — the individual student — and work upward. Each level is enabled by the data at the level below it. The same individual meal log that produces a personalised recommendation card for a student feeds the class scatter plot, which feeds the school heatmap, which feeds the Ministry of Education evidence base. One data collection architecture, four levels of simultaneous value. Point to the policy level at the top and note: this is where school nutrition programme budgets are justified — with evidence, for the first time.

**Transition:** "The most important insight is at the individual level — and the numbers speak clearly."

---

## Slide 12 — Regular Breakfast Correlates with 12% Higher Assessment Scores *(Key Finding #1)*

Lead with the number: twelve percent. Say it before you explain it. Let it register. Then unpack it: this is not an average from a journal article about a study population. This is a statistic about a named student — Kavya — in a real classroom, in a specific week. The Pearson correlation is not surfaced as a p-value for the teacher. It is surfaced as plain language: "This student tends to score 12% higher on assessments in weeks when breakfast is logged five or more times." That translation from statistical output to human-readable insight is itself an innovation. Point to the bar chart: notice that the directional effect holds across every subject. That consistency is the story — not the size of any individual bar.

**Transition:** "But correlation alone is not enough. SNPOS also detects when a student is heading toward crisis."

---

## Slide 13 — Two Concurrent Risk Flags Trigger a Welfare Alert Within Four Hours *(Key Finding #2)*

Ask the room directly: "How many students in a typical Sri Lankan school are in the Red tier right now?" Pause. Nobody knows. SNPOS would know — and would have notified the teacher four hours ago. This is the most emotionally resonant finding for a teacher or school administrator in the audience. The three-tier severity model is a deliberate design choice: requiring two concurrent risk conditions to trigger an alert reduces false-alarm fatigue while maintaining genuine sensitivity to combined nutritional and academic risk. Read the five risk conditions aloud; they are the conditions that define what "at risk" means in this system.

**Transition:** "These findings raise important questions about how we interpret and use this data responsibly."

---

## Slide 14 — SNPOS Augments Teacher Judgement — It Does Not Replace It *(Discussion)*

Pre-empt the causation challenge before anyone in the audience raises it. Say it yourself: "We want to be clear — correlation is not causation." This is an act of intellectual honesty that builds more credibility than any other single statement in the presentation. The Early Warning flag does not diagnose a student. It initiates a welfare conversation. The teacher — who knows the student, the family, the context — makes the judgement. SNPOS provides the signal; the teacher provides the interpretation. That division of responsibility is a feature, not a limitation.

**Transition:** "The implications of this system extend to every level of the school community."

---

## Slide 15 — Every Stakeholder Gains Specific, Measurable Value from SNPOS *(Implications)*

Read the table row by row. For each stakeholder, make brief eye contact with the relevant people in your audience — teachers, administrators, policy representatives. For the National/Policy row, slow down: "Scalable to 10,000+ concurrent users" is not a technical specification. It is a statement that SNPOS is not a classroom pilot — it is a national infrastructure candidate. The Ministry of Education EMIS integration pathway means SNPOS can eventually feed into the country's central education data infrastructure. That is a policy argument, not an academic one.

**Transition:** "To be credible, a system must also be honest about what it cannot yet do."

---

## Slide 16 — Five Known Constraints Strengthen Rather Than Weaken the Design *(Limitations)*

Frame this slide explicitly as intellectual rigour, not apology. Say: "We are going to tell you exactly what this system cannot do yet — and what we plan to do about it." Notice that every limitation has a named mitigation, either already committed to in a versioned release or explicitly acknowledged as ongoing work. An audience of academics or innovation judges will probe these constraints regardless. Presenting them proactively, with mitigations attached, demonstrates the kind of honest self-assessment that distinguishes mature product thinking from promotional material.

**Transition:** "Those mitigations are already planned. Here is the roadmap."

---

## Slide 17 — Four Versioned Releases Scale SNPOS to a National Platform *(Future Work)*

Walk through the timeline version by version, but do not rush. The most important point to land is why machine learning comes in Version 2.0 and not Version 1.0: "ML models require large, clean training datasets. We are building that dataset now. The AI layer is credible precisely because the data infrastructure to support it is being built first." This is a sophisticated product strategy argument that will resonate with any technology-literate judge. End on the Digital Twins framing — it elevates SNPOS from a school app to a contribution to the emerging field of digital education modelling.

**Transition:** "Which brings us to the single most important question remaining: what happens next?"

---

## Slide 18 — Sri Lanka's Students Need SNPOS Deployed in 2026 *(Conclusion)*

End with the specific ask, stated clearly and without hedging: "Deploy SNPOS in Sri Lankan secondary schools, beginning 2026." Do not say "we hope to" or "we plan to." Say "the system is ready." Then summarise the six proof points in the callout chips — 800+ foods, 4-hour alert, +12% score, PDPA compliance, 3G-ready, national pathway. After stating the ask, thank the audience and open for questions. The final thing the audience hears should be the call to action — not the formality. The closing line should feel like a natural conclusion, not an ending.
